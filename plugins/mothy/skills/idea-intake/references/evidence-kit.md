# Evidence Kit — putting the prototype in front of reality

## Who to show it to

Suggest 2-3 people, drawn from OpsHub contacts and org knowledge:
- an internal SME closest to the pain (instructor, delivery lead, operations)
- someone who would have to build or support it (an engineer or PM — informational, not a
  request for engineering time)
- the most likely sponsor (a PM, department head, or the account owner whose customer has
  the pain)

Sketch-tier prototypes are internal-only: friendly-customer exposure happens later, after a
sponsor advances the idea and Alpha exposure rules apply.

## The three questions to ask each viewer

1. "What problem do you think this solves?" (tests whether the frame communicates — don't
   pre-explain)
2. "Would you (or your customer) actually use this? When, in what moment?" (tests pull, not
   politeness)
3. "What would have to be true for this to be worth building for real?" (surfaces the
   assumptions and objections that feed the PR/FAQ's 'what might disappoint' and 'most
   contentious' answers)

Tell the submitter: write down what people DO (lean in, grab the mouse, forward it) apart
from what they SAY. Offer to mine the Granola transcript of any demo call afterward.

## The verdict rubric

- **Pursue**: at least one viewer showed pull (asked for it, named a customer, offered to
  sponsor) AND the riskiest assumption survived contact. → Idea Dossier + pr-faq.
- **Pivot**: the pain is real but the solution or persona missed. Reframe, re-prototype —
  it costs days. Update the Problem Frame's [UNKNOWN]s with what was learned.
- **Park**: no pull, or the riskiest assumption failed. Record the dossier anyway with the
  verdict and reasons — a cheaply killed idea is a success story ("engineering dollars not
  spent"), and the frame is institutional memory for whoever hits this pain next.
