## Press Release structure (≤ 1.5 pages)

1. **Headline**: [MOTH+FLAME] ANNOUNCES [SERVICE/PRODUCT] TO ENABLE [CUSTOMER SEGMENT] TO
   [BENEFIT STATEMENT]
2. **Subtitle**: one sentence — who the customer is and the benefit they gain.
3. **Dateline paragraph**: "New York, NY. - [future launch date] -- Moth+Flame, the leading
   provider of immersive learning experiences for military, government, and private
   enterprise, announced [X], a new product offering that [benefit]." Plus ~3 crisp sentences
   expanding the title.
4. **Customer Problems**: top 3-4 (max) problems / jobs-to-be-done, in the customer's own
   words where transcripts provide them. They must resonate with a customer reading them.
5. **Solution heading + body**: a few words naming the solution at work; brief overview of
   how it works; then address each listed problem — every problem maps to a mechanism
   (numbered). Link the demo video instead of restating what it shows.
6. **Leader quote**: why the company tackled this and how the solution solves it, at a high
   level. AI-drafted quotes must be approved by the named leader before leaving Draft.
7. **Additional Information / Getting started**: how easy it is to start; enough detail to
   give confidence it solves the problem.
8. **Customer quote**: `"[ILLUSTRATIVE — fictional quote ...]" -- [Fictional First & Last],
   [Role] at [Fictional/Generic Organization]`. Never a real customer, unit, office, or
   plausible real-sounding name.
9. **Call to action**: where to learn more / get access.

## Prototype Evidence section

- Prototype and demo video links (Sketch-tier prototypes are internal-only)
- What the prototype proved (3-5 bullets: what worked, who saw it, what they said)
- What the prototype disproved or changed in this document
- Real customer conversations behind the problem statements — HUMAN-WRITTEN, never AI-filled;
  "none yet" is an acceptable and meaningful answer (it means the doc is still hypothesis)

## FAQ set — DERIVED at drafting time, not copied

Always present (the honesty trio):
1. What might disappoint the customer?
2. What is the most contentious aspect of your product?
3. Why is this a problem that needs to be solved right now?

Everything else is chosen because THIS product will actually face the question. Candidate
pool to weigh (include only what applies; add what the evidence surfaces):
- Who is your customer persona and market segment?
- How does this integrate with existing Moth+Flame product features?
- What ROI can customers expect, by organization size and price point? (worked model — see
  roi-table-guide.md)
- AI-powered → What accuracy and reliability is actually achievable, and how will we prove
  it? (dual success metrics + eval plan)
- Government segment → data dependencies and update cadence; internal-platform-data-only
  fallback; funding path (web-research CURRENT appropriations/NDAA provisions at drafting
  time — never reuse stale funding answers); channel partners (Accenture/Deloitte/PwC or
  whoever is actually relevant to this deal space now)
- Whatever objections came up in customer transcripts, sponsor reactions, and prototype
  demos — these are the best questions because someone already asked them.

The exemplar's FAQ set is a historical snapshot, not a checklist. Derive, don't inherit.

## Reuse rule (goes in the doc)

Nothing in the document may be reused in external-facing material without stripping or
replacing illustrative quotes and unverified claims.
