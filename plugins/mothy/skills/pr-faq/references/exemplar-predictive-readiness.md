MOTH+FLAME ANNOUNCES PREDICTIVE READINESS ANALYTICS TO ENABLE
GOVERNMENT ORGANIZATIONS TO IMPROVE OPERATIONAL EFFICIENCY

New York, NY. - July 1, 2025 -- Moth+Flame, the leading provider of immersive learning
experiences for military, government, and private enterprise, announced Predictive Readiness
Analytics, a groundbreaking solution that leverages immersive learning data to predict and
address organizational readiness and performance risks. This innovative tool allows military and
government organizations to prevent costly issues like PTSD, suicide, and sexual assault while
improving productivity and reducing turnover rates.

Customer Problems:

   ●​ Mental Health: Identifying early indicators of PTSD, suicidal tendencies, and other
      resiliency challenges that threaten readiness and retention.
   ●​ Turnover: Reducing high attrition rates by proactively identifying recruits or personnel
      who are not suitable for their roles.
   ●​ Productivity: Improving overall productivity by addressing absenteeism and
      communication issues stemming from high-stress situations.
   ●​ Data Utilization: Ensuring practitioners can effectively use data on resiliency-related
      matters.

Solution at Work:

Predictive Readiness Analytics utilizes immersive learning data, such as conversational
scenarios, to identify risk factors in individuals and teams, providing a real-time understanding of
their mental health and performance potential. It works by:

   1.​ Analyzing Behavioral Data: Tracks metrics like decision-making efficiency, adaptability,
       and communication patterns to predict potential challenges.
   2.​ Identifying Early Warning Signs: Detects early indicators of stress, hesitation, and
       other behavioral patterns correlated with readiness risks.
   3.​ Actionable Insights: Generates a comprehensive risk profile that prescribes specific
       recommendations to mitigate issues at various levels of the organizational hierarchy
       before they escalate.
“Readiness and performance are crucial to government organizations, particularly in high-stress
environments. With Predictive Readiness Analytics, we aim to provide actionable insights that
protect personnel, reduce turnover, and improve productivity, helping organizations optimize
their resources,” said Kevin Cornish, Founder/CEO at Moth+Flame.

Getting Started:

Customers can seamlessly integrate Predictive Readiness Analytics into their existing training
and assessment framework. It works in tandem with the immersive learning platform, collecting
data and delivering predictive insights through a user-friendly dashboard. Leaders and
practitioners have access to a chat-based interface where they can get additional advice
tailored to their organization’s situation based on the data that have been collected. This
solution can be easily adapted to specific organizational needs, ensuring personalized risk
profiles and proactive recommendations.

“Predictive Readiness Analytics transformed our assessment process. It provided clear insights
into potential mental health and performance challenges, allowing us to intervene proactively.
This ultimately helped us reduce turnover and maintain high readiness,” said Barry Walsh,
Program Manager at U.S. Navy Afloat Training Group (Pacific Fleet).

How could we format an efficacy study that proves the accuracy of our predictive model?

   1.​ Define Cohorts: Select a diverse cohort of participants across various military units or
       government organizations.
   2.​ Initial Assessment: Conduct initial assessments using the Predictive Readiness Analytics
       platform to establish baseline metrics and risk predictions.
   3.​ Longitudinal Analysis: Track participants over a period of 6-12 months, comparing the
       predicted risk levels with actual incidents or performance outcomes.
   4.​ Statistical Evaluation: Analyze the predictive model's accuracy using statistical
       techniques (e.g., ROC curves, precision-recall) to compare predictions with actual
       incidents.
   5.​ Control Group: Include a control group that doesn't use the predictive model to validate
       its added value.

What types of government data would need to be accessed and how often?

   1.​ Incident Reports: Data on previous incidents of mental health crises, suicide attempts,
       and sexual assaults to train and refine the predictive models.
   2.​ Operational Metrics: Data regarding unit readiness and performance levels, to correlate
       model predictions with actual operational outcomes.
   3.​ Personnel Information: Demographics, training outcomes, and historical attrition rates
       to help segment and personalize risk assessments.

How often: Quarterly data updates are sufficient to ensure predictive models remain accurate
while maintaining data integrity and compliance.

What could we do without access to data other than what our platform can generate?

If external data access is limited, the platform can still generate valuable insights by:

   1.​ Internal Data: Leveraging the conversational data collected from training scenarios
       (e.g., hesitation, tone, eye movement) to develop internal benchmarks for readiness and
       mental health risk.
   2.​ Surveys: Collecting self-reported metrics through surveys or self-assessments on
       confidence, stress, and morale levels.
   3.​ Behavioral Comparisons: Comparing trainees' behavioral patterns with historical best
       practices identified within the platform itself.
